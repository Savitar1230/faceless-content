# Script: Every Bottle Starts Upside Down

`[n]` points to the row in `facts.md`. `video.json` holds the final text.

## Hook

- Every bottle starts its life *upside down*. [16]
- Seconds after it's made, it goes straight *back into the heat*. [14, 24]
- A big machine can make nearly *eight bottles a second*. [14]
- To see why, follow one bottle, from a pile of sand to your hand.

## The Recipe

- It begins as sand, about *70%* of the mix. [1]
- But pure silica melts at about *1,700 °C*, far too hot and too thick to work. [2]
- So soda ash goes in, and it drags the melting point *dramatically down*. [3]
- There's a catch. Soda makes glass *dissolve in water*. [3]
- A bottle that dissolves is useless, so limestone goes in to lock the glass. [4]
- Last comes *cullet*: crushed old glass that cuts the furnace's energy bill. [5]
- Every 10% of it saves about *2.5 to 3%* of the energy. [5]
- Machines weigh every part, because the recipe has to be exact. [6]

## The Furnace

- The mix rides into a furnace that *never stops*. [7]
- It runs around the clock, all year, for *eight to ten years* at a stretch. [7, 8]
- Inside, the glass reaches about *1,600 °C*, hot enough to glow almost white. [9]
- As the mix melts, it fills with tiny *gas bubbles*. [10]
- Leave them in, and they end up in your bottle. [10]
- So chemicals like sodium sulfate release gas that helps clear them out. [10]
- The clean glass slides into a long channel called the *forehearth*. [11]
- There it cools to about *1,100 °C*, stiff enough to hold a shape. [11]

## The Gob

- Now the stream gets cut. [12]
- Steel shears slice it into measured lumps called *gobs*. [12]
- Each gob is exactly one bottle, so its weight has to be right. [12]
- Gravity alone carries it down through chutes toward the machine. [12]
- That machine has up to *twenty sections*, each making up to four bottles at once. [13]

## The Flip

- And this is where it gets strange. [15, 16]
- The first mould is *upside down*. The neck gets made first, at the bottom. [15, 16]
- A metal plunger *rises* into the soft glass. [15]
- It presses the neck and a hollow starter shape called a *parison*. [15]
- That plunger has to be heatproof, and thin enough to fit a narrow neck. [18]
- Pressing keeps the walls even, which is how bottles got so light. [19]
- Now the flip.
- An arm grips the neck and swings the parison over, a full *180°*. [16, 17]
- It lands right way up, inside a second mould. [16, 17]
- The halves close, and *compressed air* blows in through the neck. [13, 16]
- The soft glass swells out until it hits the metal walls and takes their shape. [16]
- The mould opens, and there it is: a bottle, made in *seconds*. [14]

## Back Into The Heat

- It looks finished. It isn't.
- The glass has just dropped from about 1,100 to *600 °C* in around *ten seconds*. [20]
- The outside cooled fast. The inside stayed hotter. That fight is now locked in as *stress*. [20]
- Left like this, the bottle is weak, and it can crack all by itself. [21]
- First it passes under a hood of *tin vapour*, which leaves a very thin film. [22, 23]
- That film gives the next coating something to grip. [23]
- Then it goes straight back into the heat, into a long oven called a *lehr*. [24]
- The lehr heats it to about *580 °C*, and the stress starts to let go. [24]
- Then it cools slowly, over *20 to 60 minutes*, depending on how thick the glass is. [24]
- That slow walk through the heat is what keeps it from breaking. [21, 24]

## The Test

- Now it has to pass. [26]
- A waxy coating goes on, so bottles slide past each other without scratching. [25]
- Then every single bottle is inspected. [26]
- Machines hunt for tiny cracks called *checks*, plus stones, bubbles and thin walls. [26]
- Each bottle carries a mould number, so a flaw can be traced to the exact mould. [27]
- Anything that fails is rejected. The rest are packed and shipped. [26]

## The Payoff

- So this is the bottle in your hand.
- Made upside down, flipped over and blown into shape, all in seconds. [14, 16]
- Then sent back through the heat, just so it wouldn't break. [24]
- Clean glass can be melted into new bottles again and again. [5]
- So when this one comes back as cullet, it starts all over.
- Upside down.
